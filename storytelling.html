3. Reading: The Calm After the Storm

Paragraph 1: Trapped Indoors by the Weather. It had been pouring with rain all weekend, so our family spent nearly forty-eight hours indoors trying to entertain the children. Board games and drawing had lost their charm by Sunday afternoon, and everyone was desperate for fresh air. Then, unexpectedly, the storm clouds broke, and warm afternoon sunshine lit up the bay. We immediately grabbed our beach gear, jumped into the car, and headed down to the sea.

Paragraph 2: An Astonishing Sight at the Shore. When we arrived, it was obvious that dozens of local residents had had the exact same idea; the shoreline was completely packed. However, nobody was swimming or sunbathing. Instead, hundreds of people were crowded at the water’s edge, staring out to sea in amazement and picking odd items out of the receding tide.

Paragraph 3: The Cargo Mystery Revealed. My kids rushed down to the foam with excitement. It turned out that a massive commercial container ship had lost part of its cargo during Saturday night's storm. Five shipping containers of brand-new trainers had burst open against the reef, and thousands of floating athletic shoes had washed up along the sand.

Paragraph 4: The Great Shoe Search. What followed was one of the strangest scenes our coastal town had ever witnessed. Everyone was wandering along the beach, clutching odd left or right shoes and shouting out sizes, searching for someone who held the matching pair to make a set. A frustrating weekend that started with dreary rain ended up as an unforgettable communal adventure.

4. Grammar: Narrative Tenses in Storytelling

Narrative Tenses Rules: Past Simple, Continuous & Perfect

When telling a story or writing a blog post, we combine three past tenses:

• Past Simple: For consecutive completed actions in chronological order.
We grabbed our gear, jumped into the car, and drove to the beach.

• Past Continuous: For continuous background actions happening at a specific moment.
The rain was pouring, and hundreds of people were staring out to sea.

• Past Perfect Simple (had + V3): For an earlier event that happened before the main storyline.
A cargo ship had lost its containers before the shoes washed ashore.

5. Speaking: Storytelling & Personal Anecdotes
Task 5.1: Two Truths and a Lie (Anecdote Game)
Prepare three brief anecdotes about surprising or embarrassing events that happened to you or people you know (two true stories and one invented lie):
  • Use standard storytelling openers: "Did I ever tell you about...?", "I remember once...", "All of a sudden..."
  • Tell all three stories to your tutor. Your tutor must guess which anecdote is untrue.
Task 5.2: The Unforgettable Weekend (Blog Narrative)
Deliver a 2-minute spoken narrative describing a memorable trip or weekend:
  • Background: Describe what the weather was like and what you were originally planning to do.
  • Inciting incident: Introduce the unexpected complication or surprise using "All of a sudden..." or "The next thing was...".
  • Resolution: Conclude by explaining how the situation ended and how you felt (e.g. exhausted, relieved, delighted).
